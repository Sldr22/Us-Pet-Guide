Everyday grooming habits that help pets feel comfortable

Gentle, regular handling can help you notice changes early and make grooming more familiar. The right routine depends on your pet's coat, breed, age, and health.

Brushing

Use a brush suitable for your pet's coat type. Start with short, calm sessions and reward relaxed behavior. Stop if brushing causes pain, distress, or skin irritation.

Paws and nails

Check paws for stuck debris, cuts, or swelling. Nail trimming can be tricky because cutting too short may cause bleeding. Ask a veterinarian or professional groomer to demonstrate if you are unsure.

Ears and eyes

Look for unusual discharge, redness, odor, or repeated scratching. Do not put cotton swabs or unapproved products into the ear canal. Seek veterinary advice if you notice a problem.

Make it positive

Keep sessions brief, use treats if appropriate, and let your pet take breaks. For severe matting or painful skin problems, consult a veterinarian or qualified groomer rather than trying to remove it yourself.

This article is general educational information, not veterinary advice.
